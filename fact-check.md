## C. Copy decisions (from the report, accepted)

- 1.2: Black Americans "bring home," not "spend," $1.6T.
- 1.4: "Most clothes lose value. A few rare pieces, like some Hermès bags, are exceptions."
- 2.1: "Inflation used to mean creating too much money... more money is often the cause."
- 2.2: cars lose 15–20% in year one and about half by year five.
- 2.5: 43% of Black adults vs. 71% of white adults would cover $400 with cash or its equivalent (2024).
- 3.5: "13 Mississippi cities, 2 of 3,229 VA-backed loans (1947)."
- 3.7: Congress (not the trustees) loosened the Freedman's Bank charter in 1870. Trustee Henry Cooke steered assets toward his family's bank.
- 4.2: 1933 = Americans turned in their gold; 1971 = foreign governments could no longer swap dollars for gold.
- 5.2: present gold as a "what if." Bullion was illegal for Americans to own from 1933 to 1974.
- 6.2: TARP paid out $443.5B, most of it repaid; net cost $31.1B. Argue moral hazard and who got access, not the total.
- 6.3/6.4: Toys "R" Us stays (with neutral wording); replace Bed Bath & Beyond with Payless, Shopko or Joann.
- 8.1: "Prices rose about 4% a year; the money supply grew about 6–7% a year."
- 8.4: you can fly with gold but must declare it. The "illegal to carry 1kg" line is cut.
- 8.5: "Hard to confiscate when you hold your own keys." The "never" line is cut.
- 8.6: HELOC freezes cited for 2008 only.
- 10.2: use the public-domain Cornell broadside (1860), not the unverified Berkeley image.
- 10.3: Chris Rock: use the *Kill the Messenger* (2008) dentist bit, paraphrased, or drop the attribution.
- 11.3: 21M ÷ 8.2B = 1 in 390 people (0.26%).
- 11.4: tiers are Shrimp, Crab, Fish, Shark, Whale.
- 11.7: Bitcoin Ekasi is "in a South African township," not Black-led. Mention El Salvador's 2025 rollback.
